# Eidyria
Eidyria is a continent bordered by the [[Old Sea]] to the north, the [[Kande Ocean]] to the west, the [[Great Sea]] and the [[Sea of Embers]] to the south, and the [[Lazores Ocean]] to the east.

Politically, Eidyria is divided into about seven sovereign states, of which the [[Lavent-Neshian Empire]] is the largest and most populous, comprising around 23% of the population. Eidyria is estimated to have a total population of approximately five million people.

The continent includes the subregions of the North (comprising the [[Kingdom of Berentsven]] and the [[North Eidyrian Confederation]]), Western Eidyria (comprising the [[Lavent-Neshian Empire]]), and Central Eidyria (comprising the [[Kingdom of Seneaux]], the [[Union of Kinot]], [[Asfällia]], the [[Kingdom of Dagonti]], and [[Könika]]).

## Etymology
The name Eidyria comes from Old Common meaning "Our land."

## History
### Before the First Era (BFE)
Ancient [[Asfällia]] was the founding culture of Eidyrian history. Most of what is known from before the First Era can be attributed to ancient elvish texts. The original archive was destroyed during the burning of [[Tilsit Rein]] in 20,000 BFE, when a dragon raid consumed the Library of Rein, resulting in the loss of an unknown portion of the historical record. As for the rest of the continent, outside of ancient Asfällian records, artifacts provide an incomplete and inexact history as the other races did not possess written language until around 3,000 BFE.

The peoples of Eidyria have many creation myths, some of which assert that they have been present on the land since its creation, but there is no verifiable evidence to confirm these claims. The traditional theory is that the elves of ancient Asfällia were the first peoples to inhabit the continent followed shortly thereafter by the dwarves. Both races predate the burning of Tilsit Rein so it is unknown exactly for how long they have existed, though artifacts suggest that they were present for at least 10,000 years prior. The next peoples that are believed to have appeared on the continent are the dragonborn. Elven battle records from 12,000 BFE first mention encounters with dragons in the humanoid form during dragon raids from the west. As for the remaining peoples, fossil records indicate that orcs began appearing around 8,000 BFE on the [[Viden Peninsula]], humans around 6,000 BFE in the mountains of the [[Anad Peninsula]], and gnomes and halflings around 5,000 BFE along the coast of the [[Gulf of Kinot]].

The Asfällian city-state was the fundamental political unit of ancient Asfällia. The continent's first democratic system of government was instituted in Tilsit Rein in 20,000 BFE after the city's near destruction. Ancient Asfällian society was predominantly elven, though isolated dwarven communities were present in the mountainous interior. Relations between the two peoples are poorly understood during this period, but evidence suggests both maintained organized settlements and developed advanced metallurgy, architecture, and systems of writing long before the appearance of other literate cultures. Because both races predate the oldest documents, their precise origins remain unknown.

Dragons are believed to have inhabited southern Eidyria, in what is now the [[Kingdom of Dagonti]], long before the earliest surviving written records, and their precise origins are unknown. They frequently raided neighboring regions, initially acting alone, but beginning around 12,000 BFE they were accompanied by dragonborn retainers. It is widely believed that the dragonborn were created by dragons as a servitor race to perform tasks more suited to humanoid form. The political organization of draconic society remains uncertain, though most historians believe the region was divided among competing dragons, each commanding subordinate dragonborn populations.

On the rest of the continent, the people were divided into many different polities, ranging from small bands of a few families to large chiefdoms. They lived in several culture areas, which roughly correspond to geographic and biological zones that defined the representative cultures and lifestyles of the peoples who lived there. Peoples of the same race did not always share the same material culture, however, and were not necessarily always allies. These people largely descend from three distinct lineages: the orcs, who migrated east as semi-nomadic pastoralists and hunters; the gnomes and halflings, who were sedentary farmers; and the humans, who migrated south as a mixture of hunter-gatherers, herders, and early agriculturalists, eventually becoming the dominant population across much of the continent.

### First Era (1E)
The First Era conventionally begins with the collapse of draconic rule in southern Eidyria. According to both Asfällian records and later dragonborn traditions, a widespread uprising of the dragonborn against their draconic masters, aided by military intervention from [[Asfällia]], led to a prolonged and devastating conflict lasting four centuries. The revolution is believed to have resulted in the disappearance of the dragons and the irreversable destruction of the southern ecosystem, transforming much of the region into the [[Dovii Desert]]. The dragons are believed to have become extinct in the aftermath, while the surviving dragonborn were left as scattered clans across the newly formed desert. Some dragonborn sects maintain that the dragons did not become extinct, but instead fled across the [[Sea of Embers]] to unknown lands in the southeast. The end of draconic dominion marked a major turning point in Eidyrian history and serves as the chronological starting point of the First Era.

During the dragonborn uprising, the elves of sought access to dwarven mines and forges in order to produce the arms and armor necessary to wage war against the dragons. When the dwarves of [[Falsruun]] refused to provide material support, disputes over resources escalated into open conflict in 369 BFE. The war ended in a negotiated settlement that preserved dwarven autonomy while integrating Falsruun into the Asfällian state. Since that time, Falsruun has remained a semi-autonomous dwarven city under Asfällian sovereignty.

Human populations expanding through northern and central Eidyria gradually formed permanent settlements and organized kingdoms throughout the 4th and 5th centuries. In the northwest, the [[Kingdom of Berentsven]] emerged as the oldest of the northern realms consolidating control over the mountainous regions of the [[Anad Peninsula]]. To its east and south, the [[Kingdom of Seymon]], the [[Kingdom of Talov]], and the [[Kingdom of Belaya]] developed as independent monarchies linked by shared language and culture. Farther south, the [[Kingdom of Seneaux]] arose in the fertile central hills and grew into a major center of trade and political influence. Together, these kingdoms established the principal human states that would dominate the politics of northern and central Eidyria throughout the remainder of the First Era.

During the first centuries of First Era, human tribes migrating south entered the lands of the present-day [[Kingdom of Dagonti]]. They encountered a region devastated by centuries of warfare and inhabited by scattered dragonborn clans struggling to survive in the newly formed desert. Early human settlers are believed to have cooperated with the dragonborn in rebuilding settlements and establishing trade routes across the region. Over time, continued migration from the north caused humans to outnumber the native dragonborn, laying the demographic and political foundations for the later 6th century Kingdom of Dagonti.

Concurrently, human seafarers sailing south along the western coast began settling the [[Viden Peninsula]], then inhabited by orcish tribes. Early attempts of overland migration from the northeast were impractical due to the harsh climate and fierce resistance from established orcish chiefdoms. As a result, early human settlement was confined to fortified coastal enclaves that could be supplied and reinforced by sea. Initially limited to isolated coastal settlements, the newcomers gradually expanded inland through migration, trade, and warfare. Over the following centuries, the human population grew steadily and began to supplant the orcs, who were progressively displaced eastward. These human settlements on the peninsula would eventually form into the northern [[Kingdom of Lavencia]] and southern [[Kingdom of Neshium]] in the 6th and 7th centuries.

### Second Era (2E)
The onset of the Second Era is marked by the [[Infernal Reaping]] in 1E 896, a sudden and unexplained event in which humans across Eidyria developed infernal characteristics and became the first tieflings. The transformation appeared to occur simultaneously among unrelated families across all regions, with no apparent pattern regarding geography or social status. Accounts describe a brief but terrifying and painful transformation for several tens or hundreds of thousands of individuals, representing an estimated one percent of the continent's population. No consensus exists regarding the cause of the phenomenon, which remains one of the most enduring mysteries in Eidyrian history.

In the years that followed, affected families were frequently regarded with suspicion and fear. Many believed that those who had undergone the transformation had commited some unknown transgression and were being or punished by infernal forces. Tieflings were often ostracized from their communities, denied positions of authority, and subjected to legal and social discrimination. Although attitudes varied by region and period, the stigma associated with the Infernal Reaping persists to the present day.

In 2E 115, the [[Kingdom of Lavencia]] and the [[Kingdom of Neshium]] were united under a single monarch, forming the [[Lavent-Neshian Empire]]. The union brought together the two principal human states of the [[Viden Peninsula]], which had developed from the earliest coastal settlements established during the First Era. The newly formed empire rapidly emerged as the dominant political and military power in western Eidyria, consolidating authority over the peninsula and laying the foundations for centuries of imperial expansion. The formation of the empire is widely regarded as one of the most significant political developments of the Second Era.

The Lavent-Neshian Empire invaded [[Könika]] in 2E 155, inhabited by displaced orcish tribes from the Viden Peninsula over centuries of human migration. The conflict lasted for ten years and was marked by prolonged campaigning across the region's harsh badlands and dry plains. In 2E 165, the empire secured control over the territory and established a military occupation, incorporating Könika into its sphere of influence. Imperial rule accelerated the settlement of human populations and further marginalized the orcs, many of whom were displaced into neighboring regions.

In 2E 157, the [[North Eidyrian Confederation]] was formed by the [[Kingdom of Seymon]], the [[Kingdom of Talov]], and the [[Kingdom of Belaya]] in response to the ongoing invasion of Könika. The alliance was established for mutual defense and economic integration intended to deter foreign influence and invasion. Although each member state retained its own monarch and internal administration, the confederation coordinated foreign policy and military planning through an elected high king.
