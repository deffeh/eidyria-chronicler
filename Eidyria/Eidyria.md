Eidyria is a continent bordered by the [[Old Sea]] to the north, the [[Kande Ocean]] to the west, the [[Great Sea]] and the [[Sea of Embers]] to the south, and the [[Lazores Ocean]] to the east.

Politically, Eidyria is divided into about seven sovereign states, of which the [[Lavent-Neshian Empire]] is the largest and most populous, comprising about 20% of the population. Eidyria is estimated to have a total population of over five million people.

The continent includes the subregions of the North (comprising the [[Kingdom of Berentsven]] and the [[North Eidyrian Confederation]]), Western Eidyria (comprising the [[Lavent-Neshian Empire]]), and Central Eidyria (comprising the [[Kingdom of Seneaux]], the [[Union of Kinot]], [[Asfällia]], the [[Kingdom of Dagonti]], and [[Könika]]).

## Etymology
The name Eidyria comes from Old Common meaning "Our land."

## History
### Before the First Era (BFE)
Ancient [[Asfällia]] was the founding culture of Eidyrian history. Most of what is known from before the First Era can be attributed to ancient elvish texts, with the oldest dating back to 20,000 BFE. Around that time, much of the original archive was destroyed during the burning of [[Tilsit Rein]], when a dragon raid consumed the Library of Rein, resulting in the loss of an unknown portion of the historical record. As for the rest of the continent, outside of ancient Asfällian records, artifacts provide an incomplete and inexact history as the other races did not possess written language until around 3,000 BFE.

The peoples of Eidyria have many creation myths, some of which assert that they have been present on the land since its creation, but there is no verifiable evidence to confirm these claims. The traditional theory is that the elves of ancient [[Asfällia]] were the first peoples to inhabit the continent followed shortly thereafter by the dwarves. Both races predate the burning of [[Tilsit Rein]] so it is unknown exactly for how long they have existed, though artifacts suggest that they were present for at least 10,000 years prior. The next peoples that are believed to have appeared on the continent are the dragonborn. Elven records from 12,000 BFE first mention encounters with dragons in the humanoid form during dragon raids. As for the remaining peoples, fossil records indicate that orcs began appearing around 8,000 BFE on the [[Viden Peninsula]], gnomes and halflings around 6,000 BFE along the coast of the [[Gulf of Kinot]], and humans around 5,000 BFE in the mountains of the [[Anad Peninsula]].

The Asfällian city-state was the fundamental political unit of ancient Asfällia. The continent's first democratic system of government was instituted in Tilsit Rein after its near destruction in 20,000 BFE. Ancient Asfällian society was predominantly elvish, though isolated dwarven communities were present in the mountainous interior. Relations between the two peoples are poorly understood during this period, but evidence suggests both maintained organized settlements and developed advanced metallurgy, architecture, and systems of writing long before the appearance of other literate cultures. Because both races predate the oldest documents, their precise origins remain unknown.

Dragons are believed to have inhabited southern Eidyria, in what is now the [[Kingdom of Dagonti]], long before the earliest surviving written records, and their precise origins are unknown. They frequently raided neighboring regions, initially acting alone, but beginning around 12,000 BFE they were accompanied by dragonborn retainers. It is widely believed that the dragonborn were created by dragons as a servitor race to perform tasks more suited to humanoid form. The political organization of draconic society remains uncertain, though most historians believe the region was divided among competing dragons, each commanding subordinate dragonborn populations.

On the rest of the continent, the people were divided into many different polities, ranging from small bands of a few families to large chiefdoms. They lived in several culture areas, which roughly correspond to geographic and biological zones that defined the representative cultures and lifestyles of the peoples who lived there. Peoples of the same race did not always share the same material culture, however, and were not necessarily always allies.
