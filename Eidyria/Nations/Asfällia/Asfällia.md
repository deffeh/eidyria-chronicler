# Asfällia
Asfällia is a country in eastern and southern Eidyria. It lies between the [[Duns]] to the north, the [[Lazores Ocean]] to the east, and the [[Sea of Embers]] to the south. It has a total population of over 750 thousand. Asfällia borders the [[Kingdom of Seneaux]] to the north and the [[Kingdom of Dagonti]] to the west. The capital, [[Tilsit Rein]], is the largest Asfällian city, followed by [[Falsruun]] and [[Mittenmeer]].

Asfällia is considered the cradle of civilization and the birthplace of democracy and Eidyrian history. The ancient Asfällians were organized into independent city-states that spanned the land. Ardreth II of [[Tilsit Rein]] united most of present-day Asfällia, with his son Ardreth III uniting the remaining city-states and forming the High Assembly in the 15th millennium BFE. 

## Etymology
According to ancient elvish scholars, the land was named Asfällia, meaning "Eastern Mountains," by the first elves in their language, Sylvan.

## History
### Before the First Era
Ancient elves and dwarves were present in Asfällia at least 30,000 years ago. The oldest humanoid fossils on the continent belong to the elves and were discovered near Mittenmeer on the Lazores coast of Asfällia. From the coast, the elves expanded south, west, and north west where they first came into contact with the ancient dwarves. The relationship between the two peoples are unclear during this period, but evidence suggests both maintained separate settlements and independently developed advanced metallurgy, architecture, and systems of writing.

Western expansion was halted near the [[Valethir River]] due to dragons inhabiting the river valley. Dragon raids were most frequent and devastating during the first millennia of elven civilization, when sparse populations and fragmented settlements left much of the region vulnerable. The most catastrophic of these attacks was the Burning of Tilsit Rein in 20,000 BFE, during which much of the city was destroyed, including the Library of Rein where a significant portion of early Asfällian historyical records had been stored. Over the following millennia, growing populations and the development of fortified city-states gradually reduced the scale and effectiveness of such attacks. These developments included extensive fortifications, anti-dragon siege weapons, and early warning systems using a network of beacons spanning the land.

As dragon attacks ravaged much of the elven frontier, the dwarven settlements remained comparatively secure. Sheltered within heavily fortified mountain holds, the dwarves avoided many of the demographic and political disruptions that shaped the development of early elven civilization.

Following its near destruction in 20,000 BFE, Tilsit Rein entered a period of reconstruction and political unrest. Many citizens blamed the ruling aristocracy for its failure to adequately address the dragon threat, leading to widespread calls for reform and the eventual establishment of the continent's first democracy. Although initially unique to Tilsit Rein, democratic governance gradually spread across Asfällia over the following millennia as other city-states adopted similar political institutions.

As Premier of Tilsit Rein, Ardreth II is credited with the gradual political unification of much of present-day Asfällia through diplomacy, alliances, and political reform. The process was completed by his son, Ardreth III, who secured the participation of the remaining city-states and established the High Assembly in the 15th millennium BFE, laying the foundation for a unified Asfällian government.

The first recorded appearances of dragonborn occurred during the 12th millennium BFE. Dragons began appearing alongside organized bands of humanoid retainers, believed to be the earliest recorded dragonborn. Unlike the dragons that had plagued the region for millennia, dragonborn were capable of occupying territory, disrupting trade routes, and conducting prolonged military operations. Their emergence compelled Asfällia to shift from a strategy of local defense to one of fortified frontiers, permanent garrisons, and coordinated military campaigns.

Over the following millennia, dragons and their dragonborn followers came to dominate much of southern Eidyria. While Asfällia remained beyond their direct control, conflict along the western frontier became a defining feature of the period. Records from this era are limited and often contradictory, though they consistently describe numerous draconic domains ruled by rival dragons and supported by dragonborn populations.

By the final millennium of draconic rule, tensions between dragons and their dragonborn subjects had become apparent. Though the exact causes remain debated, records point to growing unrest throughout the southern domains and a gradual breakdown of relations between rulers and subjects. In the fourth century BFE, widespread rebellions erupted across the draconic domains, marking the beginning of the Dragonborn Uprising. As dragonborn forces rose against their draconic overlords, much of southern Eidyria descended into war. 

## Politics
### High Assembly
The Assembly, officially the High Assembly, is the legislative body that governs Asfällia. Members are elected based on ability and talent measured through examination and demonstrated achievement. This system allows anyone who passes an examination to become a Assembly member, a position that brings wealth and honor to the whole family.

The High Speaker is the presiding officer of the High Assembly and serves as its chief representative. The office is elected by the members of the Assembly from among their own ranks and is responsible for administering legislative proceedings, maintaining order during debate, and acting as the Assembly's official voice in matters of state. Traditionally regarded as a position of prestige and impartiality, the High Speaker is expected to place the interests of the Assembly above those of their home city or political faction while in office.

### Censorate
The Censorate is a supervisory agency established in 147 BFE. The investigating censors are "the eyes and ears" of the Assembly that monitor and investigate civilian society as well as government bureaucracy at each level to prevent corruption and malfeasance. They are in charge of investigations and duties such as gathering information and complaints from the people and impeaching government administrators for misconduct. Popular stories tell of righteous censors revealing corruption as well as censors who accept bribes.
